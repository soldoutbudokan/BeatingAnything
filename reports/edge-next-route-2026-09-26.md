- Mechanism: dispersion matters differently in a three-player minimum than a two-player comparison. At equal expected scores, a more variable player has a larger chance of being best and worst; a fixed common-variance conversion can underprice the best-score tail. Estimate shrunk, player-specific variance from earlier field-centered rounds. Hold the baseline relative means implied by the three prices fixed, change only relative variances, and calculate discrete-score probabilities including fractional tie returns.
- Cheap test: choose a fixed shrinkage before inspecting outcomes, then carry it unchanged to 2025. Compare probabilistic scoring against the unmodified price baseline, then realized return at the actual quoted opening price, clustered by event. Reconstruct result and fractional dead-heat payout from scores, rather than importing publisher model predictions. This is distinct from failed G3 tie allocation, G10 shared-weather covariance, and Sunday chasing variance. The executed test used simple shrinkage declared in the script; no training or parameter search was performed.
