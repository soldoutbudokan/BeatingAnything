## Using it

```python
from beating.cricket.competitions import competition_for, drs_available_for_matches, season_key, series_key
from beating.cricket.rules import follow_on_threshold, rules_for, rules_for_match

comp = competition_for(info)          # Competition(competition_id='ipl', tier='franchise_major', fmt='T20', event_id='ipl', ...)
season_key(info)                      # 'ipl:2023'
rules_for_match(info, match_id=match_id)    # competition, teams, stage and date come from info
r = rules_for("intl_test_m", "TEST", "male", "2021-02-05", teams=["India", "England"])
follow_on_threshold(r, days_remaining=4)    # 150 (Law 14.1: 200 / 150 / 100 / 75 for 5+ / 3-4 / 2 / 1 days)
rules_for("blast", "T20", "male", "2023-07-15", stage="Final")["tie_break"]   # 'so_repeat'

matches["series_key"] = series_key(matches)                 # ingest matches table, or any frame with ids
reviews = balls[balls.review_by.notna()].groupby("match_id").size()
matches[["drs_available", "drs_source"]] = drs_available_for_matches(matches, reviews)
```

**Fields.** `rules_for(competition_id, fmt, gender, date, *, event_id, teams, official, assoc_match, knockout, stage, scheduled_days, match_id)` returns every plan section-5 field under its plan name (`rules.PLAN_FIELDS`): `overs_per_innings`, `balls_per_over`, `bowler_quota`, `pp_windows`, `field_restr`, `free_hit`, `p_free_hit_given_nb`, `noball_penalty_runs`, `caught_crossing_counts`, `drs_allowed`, `reviews_per_innings`, `umpires_call_retains_review`, `review_topup_after_80`, `ucall_zone_era`, `test_overs_per_day`, `scheduled_days`, `follow_on_lead`, `new_ball_after_overs`, `two_new_balls`, `impact_player`, `tie_break`, `min_overs_for_result`. It adds `_layers` (the layer or table that set each field) and these extras: `balls_per_innings`, `bowler_quota_balls`, `reduced_quota_rule`, `pp_reduced_rule`, `bowler_quota_reduced`, `pp_windows_reduced`, `follow_on_lead_by_days`, `drs_available`, `drs_scope`, `two_new_balls_until_over`, `tactical_sub`, `tie_break_rule`, `min_balls_for_result`, `max_consecutive_balls_per_end`, and the informational `slow_over_rate_fielding_penalty`, `stop_clock` and `concussion_substitutes`.

The earlier names are still returned as deprecated aliases (`rules.LEGACY_ALIASES`), so older callers such as `provider.engine_rules` keep working: `noball_extra_runs` = `noball_penalty_runs`, `test_days` = `scheduled_days`, `powerplay_overs` = `pp_windows`, `fielders_outside` = `field_restr`, `bowler_quota_overs` = `bowler_quota`, `balls_in_use` (`one`, `two_new_balls`, `two_balls_until_34`) from `two_new_balls`, and `super_over` / `super_over_this_match` = `tie_break_rule` / `tie_break` with the old values `single` (for `so_boundary_count`) and `repeat` (for `so_repeat`).

**Derived per match.**

- `tie_break` is resolved for the match: `none`, `bowl_out`, `so_boundary_count` (one super over, then the boundary count, as in the 2019 World Cup final 1144530) or `so_repeat` (super overs until there is a winner). The config value `knockout_only` (kept in `tie_break_rule`) becomes `so_boundary_count` before 2019-10-14 and `so_repeat` after in knockouts, and `none` otherwise. Under deviation D5 the engines play `so_boundary_count` as the repeated super over and a bowl-out as a coin flip.
- `drs_allowed` is the plan's "rules allow DRS": true when `drs_available` (the competition default) is true or `drs_scope` is `selected`. Per-match DRS is `drs_available_for_matches` (below), never `drs_available` alone.
- `p_free_hit_given_nb` is 0 when `free_hit` is `none`, 1 for `all_noballs`, and for `front_foot` eras the data estimate (config `p_free_hit_given_nb`): Blast 0.61, One-Day Cup 0.77, men's ODIs 0.71, men's T20Is 0.75, IPL 0.71, and the pooled 0.70 for cells with fewer than 20 expected dismissals (women's internationals, the Hundred, BPL, CPL, CSA T20, the ECB women's competitions). Engine step 9.2.13 uses it as the probability that a no-ball gives a free hit.
- `follow_on_lead` is Law 14.1 for the scheduled length (200 for five days, 150 for four). `follow_on_lead_by_days` is the full table, and `follow_on_threshold(rules, days_remaining)` reads it with the days left when play first starts (Law 14.1.3), which only the simulation knows: Ireland v Pakistan 2018 (1127284) lost day 1 and the follow-on was enforced at 180 under the 150 tier.
- `ucall_zone_era` is `pre_2016`, `2016_wider` (from 2016-10-01, the stump zone widened to the outer edge of the stumps) or `2021_higher` (from 2021-04-01, raised to the top of the bails). These are anchors only: Cricsheet's umpire's-call flags start in late 2017, so the report gives the umpire's-call share of failed reviews by family and era without a test.
- `bowler_quota_reduced[str(N)]` is `[overs per bowler, bowlers allowed one more, max balls]` for every innings length N from 1 to the scheduled overs: floor(N/5) overs with N mod 5 bowlers allowed one more, and 2 each below 10 overs (`fifth_min2`); in the Hundred N/5 of the balls rounded up to whole sets, at least 10 balls (`hundred_balls`).
- `pp_windows_reduced[str(N)]` is `{mandatory, field_restr}` in 1-based legal balls of the innings, from the integer formulas in `reduced_tables` (T20: round_half_up(0.3 N) overs, in balls from July 2025 and in ECB T20 from 2018; BBL/WBBL power surge: max(2, ceil(N/5)) overs; men's ODIs from 2015: P2 = round_half_up(0.6 N), the rest split P1 = ceil, P3 = floor; other ODIs: round_half_up(N/5); the Hundred: round_half_up(B/4) balls, low confidence).
- The engine applies both tables with the N known at the time (plan 7.6, "after every revision"): an innings cut after its powerplay has ended keeps the powerplay it had, a cut during it ends the powerplay at the later of the current ball and the new table value, and a bowler already past the new quota bowls no more. Data: before-innings cuts follow the quota table in 843 of 847 T20 and 537 of 541 ODI innings (all 9 Hundred innings), mid-innings cuts in 132 of 168 and 157 of 212.

`str(comp)` is the `competition_id`, so the returned object can stand in for the id string.

## Series and per-match DRS

`series_key(matches)` (plan 4.2) returns `competition_id|event|season` for tournaments and leagues, and `competition_id|event|season|A v B|first date` for bilateral series: event ids `bilateral`, `intl_unnamed` and `intl_other`, the ICC Women's Championship (a league of bilateral series), and England's "NatWest Series" from 2006 (one name for each summer's two or three separate bilateral series). Within a group, a new series starts after a gap of more than 60 days; a tournament key gets the first date only when that splits it. On the ingest `matches` table it derives `competition_id` and `event_id` itself (`competitions_for_matches`). The October 25 build has 2,677 series keys.

`drs_available_for_matches(matches, reviews_by_match)` implements plan section 5: rules allow DRS (`drs_allowed`, computed with `rules_for` per row when the column is absent) AND (a review in the match OR in another match of the same series whose reviews are not missing) AND "reviews" is not in `info.missing`. It returns `drs_available` (true, false, or null when reviews are missing; those matches are excluded from DRS fits) and `drs_source` (`match`, `series`, `rules`, `unknown`). On the ingest tables it gives 4,843 `match`, 2,264 `series` (834 of them true), 15,873 `rules` and 152 `unknown`, the same counts as the zip-based check `drs_series` in the report. `rules.match_drs_available` is the same formula for a frame that already has `series_key`, `drs_allowed`, `has_review` and `reviews_missing`.

## Competitions

**Method.** A match with `team_type == 'international'` always maps to `intl_<t20|odi|test>_<m|w>` from the Cricsheet match type (Test/MDM -> test, ODI/ODM -> odi, T20/IT20 -> t20). The event name only sets `event_id`: `icc_cwc`, `icc_t20wc`, `icc_ct`, `icc_wtc_final`, `icc_qualifier` (any "qualifier", "region" or "play-off" event), `icc_league` (World Cricket League, CWC League 2, Challenge League), `icc_intercontinental`, `icc_womens_championship`, `asia_cup`, `multisport`, `bilateral` ("X tour of Y", "X in Y ... Series", "X v Y", named trophies such as The Ashes and Border-Gavaskar), `multi_nation` (tri-series, quadrangulars and other cups), `intl_unnamed` (no event name). `assoc_match` is true when either side was not an ICC full member on the match date. Zimbabwe counts from 1992-07-06, Bangladesh from 2000-06-26, and Ireland and Afghanistan from 2017-06-22. The World, Asia and Africa XIs count as full members. `official` is false for IT20, ODM and MDM internationals. Tier is `intl_full` for official matches between two full members and `intl_assoc` otherwise. Event names with "Rising Stars" or "Emerging" are `multi_nation` (so the Asia Cup DRS layer does not reach the associate Rising Stars matches 1509859 and 1522068), and the 2018 Trans-Tasman Twenty20 Trophy tri-series is `multi_nation`, not `bilateral`. The knockout flag (`is_knockout`) matches final, semi, quarter, qualifier N, eliminator, play-off, knockout and challenger stages, but not "Race to the Final".

A club match maps by its normalised event name through an ordered alias table. That table absorbs sponsor and format renames: Indian Premier League; (KFC) Big Bash League; NatWest T20 Blast, Vitality Blast, Vitality Blast Men, Friends Provident/Friends Life t20 and Twenty20 Cup -> `blast`; Vitality Blast Women -> `blast_w` (a bare event named "T20" is not taken for the Blast); LV=, Specsavers and Rothesay County Championship, and the Bob Willis Trophy -> `county_championship`; Royal London, Metro Bank, Clydesdale Bank 40 and Yorkshire Bank 40 One-Day Cup -> `eng_one_day_cup`; Rachael Heyhoe Flint Trophy and ECB Women's One-Day Cup -> `eng_women_one_day`; (Marsh) Sheffield Shield; The Marsh Cup, Marsh One-Day Cup, Ryobi, Matador and JLT One-Day Cup, and One-Day Cup (Australia) -> `aus_one_day_cup`; HRV Cup, HRV Twenty20, Georgie Pie, Burger King and Dream11 Super Smash -> `super_smash`; CSA T20 Challenge, Ram Slam, MiWAY and CSA Pro20 -> `csa_t20`; The Hundred Men's and Women's Competition -> `hundred_m` and `hundred_w` (`fmt = HUNDRED`). The table also maps Ranji Trophy, Vijay Hazare, Quaid-e-Azam, Ford Trophy and others that are not in this Cricsheet build. A club event that matches nothing falls back to `other_<fmt>_<m|w>` with `generic=True` and tier `domestic_minor`.

Tiers: `franchise_major` covers IPL, BBL, PSL, CPL, SA20, ILT20, MLC, BPL, LPL, Mzansi Super League, both Hundreds, WPL, WBBL, WCPL and the Women's Cricket Super League. `domestic_major` covers the national first-class, one-day and T20 championships of full members, including the Blast. `domestic_minor` covers the Irish Inter-Provincials, Women's T20 Challenge, FairBreak, Nepal Premier League, European T20 Premier League and the generic ids.

**Coverage (all 23,132 matches).** There are 1,117 distinct event names (plus matches with none) and 1,445 (name, match type, gender, team type) rows. They map to 46 competition ids: intl_full 5,306 matches, intl_assoc 5,466, franchise_major 4,872, domestic_major 7,140, domestic_minor 348. All 89 matches without an event name are internationals. They map to their `intl_*` id with `event_id = intl_unnamed`, which is the only generic mapping in this build. No club match falls back to a generic id. Cricsheet match ids are not all integers: 25 files are named `wi_2017xx` / `wi_21xxxx`, and both modules key them as strings.

## The rules timeline

`config/cricket-rules.json` is a list of layers. Each layer has a `when` filter (format, gender, team type, competition, tier, event, official, associate, `teams_any`, `teams_all`, `knockout` from the Cricsheet stage, `stage_regex`), a half-open date range `[from, until)`, the fields it sets, a `source` (`memory`, `data` or `memory+data`) and a note. Layers apply in file order, and later ones override earlier ones field by field. There are 131 layers. The order is: format defaults (including the reduced-innings rules), umpire's-call zone eras and caught-crossing dates, then international DRS by era, team and event exceptions (India 2008-16; Zimbabwe and Ireland from 2009-11; Bangladesh Tests to 2013 and ODIs to 2014; associates; women's series; ICC events and the Asia Cup), then each domestic competition. The top-level tables `follow_on_lead_by_days`, `p_free_hit_given_nb` and `reduced_tables` feed the derived fields. `match_overrides` mark the known four-day men's Tests: South Africa v Zimbabwe 2017, England v Ireland 2019 and 2023, Ireland v Zimbabwe 2024, England v Zimbabwe 2025. They also mark the five-day 2023 women's Ashes Test, the 2021 World Test Championship final (five days plus a reserve day) and, for validation only, the day lost at the start of Ireland v Pakistan 2018 (`days_lost_at_start`). `scheduled_days_rules` sets 98 overs a day for a four-day Test, and the follow-on lead follows from the days through `follow_on_lead_by_days`.

## Data versus memory: conflicts and the final choice

Data wins wherever it can see the rule. Each conflict is also noted on its layer in the config.

1. **ODI free hit start.** The task brief put the ODI front-foot free hit in 2011. Data shows the ball after a no-ball ends in a bowled/caught/lbw/stumped dismissal 1.85% of the time to September 2007 (49 of 2,653). The rate is 0.81% from October 2007 to September 2011 (7 of 863), 0.89% from October 2011 to June 2015 (6 of 676), and 0 of 2,045 afterwards. Nothing changes in 2011. **Choice:** front foot from 2007-10-01, all no-balls from 2015-07-05.
2. **The 2020 extra review did not lapse.** From memory, the extra unsuccessful review added in July 2020 was a temporary measure. Data shows 3 non-umpire's-call failures by one team in a Test innings every year from 2020 to 2026, and 2 (never 3) in ODIs and T20Is. **Choice:** Tests 3 and white-ball internationals 2 from 2020-07-01, still in force.
3. **ODI reviews in early 2011.** From memory, ODIs allowed one unsuccessful review per innings from the start of DRS. In the January 2011 Australia v England ODIs and the 2011 World Cup, 37 of 137 team-innings with a failed review used two, and none used three. From August 2011 the maximum is one. **Choice:** 2 reviews until 2011-05-01, then 1.
4. **When T20 went to two reviews.** The plan states the pooled T20 data shows "1 until 2018 and 2 from 2019". The only 2019 innings with two failures is one Quetta Gladiators innings in the PSL. Every other 2019 team-innings, 146 of 147 in club T20, has at most one. The real step is August 2020 for T20Is and competition by competition in the leagues: PSL 2021, BPL and LPL 2021/22, IPL 2022, CPL, SA20, ILT20, MLC and WPL from their DRS start. The Hundred, BBL and WBBL stay at one. **Choice:** per-competition dates from data. The PSL 2019 innings is treated as an untagged umpire's call.
5. **ECB free hits were front-foot only until 2024.** The expectation was that domestic competitions followed the 2015 ICC change to free hits for all no-balls. In the Blast, protected dismissals still happen on free-hit balls in every season from 2014 to 2024 (43 of 1,832 balls, 0.44 of the base rate). The same holds in the One-Day Cup (2015-23), the Rachael Heyhoe Flint Trophy (2022-23), the Charlotte Edwards Cup (2024) and the Hundred (2022-23), and the rate is zero from 2025. **Choice:** front foot to 2024, all no-balls from 2025. The 2014 One-Day Cup has 4 protected dismissals in 133 such balls, above the base rate, so it is set to no free hit.
6. **Two-run no-balls beyond the County Championship.** From memory, only the County Championship has 2-run no-balls. Data shows every no-ball in the Blast, the One-Day Cup and both Hundreds carries 2 extras through 2025, and all of them carry 1 in 2026. The County Championship still carries 2 in 2026. ECB women's competitions and the Women's Cricket Super League from 2017 carry 1, and the 2016 Super League carried 2. **Choice:** data.
7. **BBL free hits in BBL|01-02.** The expectation was free hits from the first season. Data shows 4 protected dismissals in 35 balls after no-balls in 2011-13 (more than the base rate) and none in about 500 since. **Choice:** no free hit until 2013/14, then all no-balls.
8. **WBBL power surge.** The expectation was the BBL power surge (2020/21) in both leagues. The WBBL records a 6-over powerplay until 2021/22 and the 4 + 2 structure from 2022/23. **Choice:** data.
9. **DRS in the Blast and SMAT.** The expectation was no DRS. Data shows reviews only in knockouts: Blast Finals Day 2022 (not the quarter-finals), all knockouts in 2023 and 2025, none in 2024 or 2026, and SMAT knockouts from 2023/24. **Choice:** `drs_scope = knockouts` for those seasons (Blast 2022 uses a semi/final stage filter).
10. **Minimum overs in English one-day cricket.** From memory, the List A minimum is 20 overs. The One-Day Cup (2014, 2015) and the women's one-day competition (2023) have D/L results from 10-over chases. **Choice:** 10.
11. **ODI super overs.** From memory, bilateral ODI ties stand and only ICC knockouts use a super over. Data shows bilateral ties went to super overs in 2020-23, the Super League and Women's Championship period (Zimbabwe v Pakistan 2020, West Indies v South Africa women 2021 and 2022). The 2023 World Cup Qualifier group tie also went to one, as did League 2 ties in 2025 and Bangladesh v West Indies in October 2025. Sri Lanka v India in 2024 stood as a tie. **Choice:** the layered timeline in the config. The 2025 men's bilateral change rests on one match (low confidence).
12. **Women's ODI powerplays.** The expectation was the men's P1/P2/P3 structure from 2015. Cricsheet records only overs 1-10 for women's ODIs, plus a 5-over batting block (mostly overs 36-40) until 2021. **Choice:** `pp_windows` follows the recorded windows. `field_restr` keeps the memory value of 4 outside in overs 11-50, so any P3 effect is not modelled (the verification believed women's ODIs now use the men's 4/5 split in overs 11-40/41-50; data cannot settle it).
13. **Super Smash, CSA T20 and Women's Cricket Super League ties.** The expectation was super overs as in the franchise leagues. Ties since 2022/23 (five in the Super Smash) and most CSA T20 and WCSL ties stood. **Choice:** `knockout_only`.
14. **Asia Cup DRS.** The expectation was ICC-event conditions. No Asia Cup review appears in 2004-2016, and they appear in almost every match from 2018. **Choice:** DRS from 2018.
15. **Women's DRS.** The expectation was DRS in women's internationals from about 2017. Reviews appear in 27% of 2017 World Cup matches and in no women's T20I before the 2018 T20 World Cup. Since 2022 they appear in 87% of ODIs and 62% of T20Is between two of Australia, England, India, New Zealand and South Africa, against 0-30% elsewhere. **Choice:** `selected` by default, with DRS on for ICC events and big-five bilateral series from 2022.
16. **Sri Lanka Major League length.** The expectation was a four-day first-class competition. 83 of 84 draws span three dates, with overs per day at p90 88. The 2025 final spans four dates. **Choice:** 3 days and 90 overs, finals 4 days.
17. **Intercontinental Cup overs per day.** There is no firm memory. Its three drawn matches reach 93 overs per day, above any five-day Test draw (maximum 90.0). **Choice:** 96 (low confidence).
18. **The Hundred's DRS in 2021.** There was no firm memory either way. Reviews appear in 72-84% of 2021 matches, with at most one failure per team-innings. **Choice:** DRS from 2021 with one review.
19. **Associates against full members.** The first version set `drs_scope = none` for every associate match outside ICC events. Data shows reviews in 34 such matches: the Netherlands' CWC Super League ODIs (2021-2023), Ireland v South Africa and Australia (2016) and in England (2017), Barbados at the 2022 Commonwealth Games, Bangladesh v Netherlands T20Is (2025) and the Super Six of the 2023 World Cup Qualifier. **Choice:** `selected` for associate v full-member internationals (men from 2016, women from November 2018) and for associate matches of the 2023 Qualifier.
20. **Caught: does crossing count?** The plan gives 2022-10-01 internationally and IPL 2022. Data agrees and adds that ECB domestic competitions (County Championship, Blast, One-Day Cup, Charlotte Edwards Cup, Rachael Heyhoe Flint) switched for the 2022 season, the Marsh Cup from September 2022, and the Hundred never let crossing count. **Choice:** data.
21. **DRS depended on the host board.** From memory, DRS was in every Test from November 2009 and every ODI from 2011 except India's. Grouping by event and series (the stronger check asked for by the verification) shows whole series without a review whenever Zimbabwe, Ireland (as a full member) or, early on, Bangladesh played at home. Outside ICC events and the Asia Cup, reviews appear in 45 of 136 Zimbabwe ODIs from 2011, 17 of 42 Zimbabwe Tests and 24 of 72 T20Is; in 29 of 51 Ireland ODIs, 20 of 49 T20Is and 7 of 11 Tests; and in 1 of 20 Bangladesh Tests to 2013 (68 of 71 from 2015) and 2 of 42 Bangladesh ODIs in 2011-14 (87 of 104 from 2016). **Choice:** `drs_scope = selected` for those teams and periods. Per-match `drs_available` then comes from the series.
22. **Women's Test reviews.** The first version gave women's Tests 3 reviews. In all 4 women's innings with a third failed review, it came after over 80, and no innings has 3 before over 80 (13 Tests). **Choice:** 2 reviews with the top-up after 80 overs from June 2021 (low confidence).
23. **Free hits in front-foot eras.** Only front-foot no-balls gave a free hit, so the share of no-balls followed by one is below 1. The estimate is 1 minus the protected-dismissal ratio on the next ball, with the expected count taken over by over (no-balls cluster in high-wicket overs), shrunk to the pooled ratio with a prior worth 10 expected dismissals. **Choice:** the estimates for the five cells with 20+ expected dismissals and the pooled 0.70 elsewhere. The simpler pooled ratio quoted in the verification (Blast 0.56, ODIs 0.67, women's T20Is 0.34 from 100 balls) moves to 0.61, 0.71 and the pooled value.
24. **Reduced-innings rounding.** There was no firm memory of the rounding in the ICC reduced-innings tables. Where floor and round half up differ, the recorded first powerplay rounds half up in 312 T20 innings against 16 rounding down (T20Is 2007, IPL 2008, WBBL 2016, BBL 2018/19), in 71 against 18 in one-powerplay ODIs and in 151 against 0 for the men's P1/P2/P3 split. Power-surge innings of 5 overs keep a 2-over powerplay. **Choice:** round half up everywhere, minimum 2 overs under the power surge; the five seasons that round down are explained anomalies. The quota rule floor(N/5) with N mod 5 bowlers one more holds in 99% of innings cut before they began.
25. **One event name, several series.** England's home ODIs were all called "NatWest Series" from 2006 to 2013 (two or three bilateral series a summer), and the ICC Women's Championship is a league of bilateral series. Keyed by event and season, a series without DRS (England v Sri Lanka 2011) would borrow reviews from another (England v India 2011). **Choice:** the series key splits both by team pair.

The data agrees with memory on these points: the 2008 Test trial with 3 reviews; the official Test start in November 2009; India's refusal of DRS until November 2016; the Test top-up after 80 overs from October 2013 to September 2017; umpire's-call retention from 2017-09-28; T20I DRS from October 2017; IPL DRS from 2018 with 2 reviews from 2022; IPL impact player from 2023 (291 of 293 matches); ILT20 impact player from 2023/24; SMAT dropping the impact player in 2024/25; ODI supersubs from July 2005 to March 2006; the BBL X-factor in 2020-22; the ODI powerplay eras of 2005, 2008, 2011, 2012 and 2015; the 6-over T20 and 25-ball Hundred powerplays; 20-ball Hundred quotas with 5- or 10-ball ends; 90 overs a day and a 200-run follow-on in five-day Tests; 100 overs a day in women's Tests; and a 150-run follow-on in four-day cricket.

## Data anomalies (kept, marked "explained" in the report)

- Ireland v Pakistan 2018 (1127284): follow-on enforced with a lead of 180 in a five-day Test. Day 1 was washed out, so Law 14.1.3 applied the four-day threshold of 150. The `days_lost_at_start` match override lets the check use the right tier; `rules_for` keeps the five-day value, since a lost day is not known before the match.
- PSL 2019 (1168827): one innings with two failed reviews against one allowed.
- Tests 892517 (Melbourne 2015) and 1104284 (Dhaka 2017): Australia credited with three failed fielding reviews before over 80, against two allowed. These are likely mis-attributed reviews.
- Marsh Cup 1322388: D/L result on a 19.5-over target.
- WBBL 2020/21: one supersub-coded replacement.
- `info.overs = 50` in 12 men's and 7 women's T20Is that have 20-over innings. Eight innings credit a bowler with 5 T20 overs or 11 ODI overs (attribution errors).
- Ties without the expected super over: Blast quarter-final 2020 (1207789), Scotland v Ireland T20I 2018 (1142504), Bangladesh v India women ODI 2023 (1382168). There is also one CSA T20 group tie with a super over (1299613).
- DRS rows pooled across series: England's 2011 NatWest Series (England v Sri Lanka without reviews, England v India with them) and the 2025 Zimbabwe T20I tri-series in Harare (no DRS, while New Zealand v South Africa there defaults to DRS). Per-match `drs_available` is right in both through the series key.
- Reduced powerplays that round down: BBL 2018/19 (two 19-over matches with 5-over powerplays), One-Day Cup 2014 (mixed), one 48-over women's ODI 2018/19 (1168164), WBBL 2022/23 (12- and 13-over innings with 2-over powerplays). The Hundred's pooled row (13 innings) is low confidence: 85- and 90-ball innings record 20-ball powerplays.
- The 2015 Blast quarter-final 804705 was decided on "Lost fewer wickets" after a tie in a rain-shortened match with no time for a super over. No `tie_break` value represents it, and the engines do not model it.
- Five SMAT results are decided by the VJD method (1205719, 1205779, 1205824, 1383434, 1383461); the minimum-overs check now includes them, and all are above the 5-over minimum.

## Limitations

- Several rules are invisible in Cricsheet: two new balls, the 2025 34-over ball change, fielders outside the circle outside recorded powerplays, the umpire's-call zone, stop clocks, slow-over-rate penalties, concussion-sub rules in domestic cricket, bouncer limits. These are marked `memory`, and the informational ones are not used by the engines.
- DRS availability is series-specific in some eras and for some hosts (India 2008-16, Zimbabwe, Ireland, Bangladesh to 2014, women's bilaterals, associates against full members). `drs_available` is only the competition default and `drs_scope = selected` flags the partial cases. Fits on history must use the per-match value from `drs_available_for_matches`. The default is per team, not per host, because `rules_for` does not see the venue, so a neutral match in a series without DRS defaults to DRS (Zimbabwe tri-series 2025).
- A series key cannot see a series whose matches carry different event names, and a multi-nation event name reused for separate series is split only for the NatWest Series and the ICC Women's Championship.
- Umpire's-call flags exist only from late 2017, so earlier review counts treat every failed review as lost. That is also the rule then, but it means a mislabelled review cannot be told apart. `ucall_zone_era` has no testable data before its first change.
- The knockout flag comes from the Cricsheet `event.stage` string, which many matches lack.
- Four-day men's Tests are listed by match id from memory. All five finished inside four dates, so the data is consistent with the list but cannot confirm it. The 2026 Ireland v New Zealand Test at Stormont (1528547) is past that memory and keeps the five-day default.
- Lost days before play starts are known only in simulation: `follow_on_lead` is the scheduled-length value, and the engine must call `follow_on_threshold` with the days remaining. Likewise the reduced tables give the rule for each N, and the engine must apply them with the N known at the time (a powerplay or quota already passed stays passed).
- The Hundred's reduced powerplay table and the BBL/WBBL power-surge table rest on 13 and 48 scored innings.
- `p_free_hit_given_nb` for small front-foot cells (women's internationals, the Hundred, ECB women's competitions) is the pooled 0.70; their own counts are consistent with it but cannot pin a separate value.
- Cricsheet folds byes and leg-byes run off a no-ball into `extras.noballs` (values 3-6 on 353 balls, mostly ECB 2014-17). Ingest must take the penalty from `noball_penalty_runs` and treat the rest as byes.
- The free-hit check compares against each competition's over-by-over base rate of protected dismissals. Even with no free hit, the ball after a no-ball is out less often than average (about 0.75 of the base rate), so the front-foot estimates may overstate the free-hit share slightly.

## Deviations from the plan

- `competition_for` returns a frozen `Competition` dataclass, not a bare string. `str()` gives the `competition_id`, and the dataclass also carries `tier`, `fmt`, `event_id`, `assoc_match`, `official` and `generic`. Unmatched club events use `other_<fmt>_<m|w>` ids, which the plan does not list.
- `rules_for` takes extra keyword arguments (`event_id`, `teams`, `official`, `assoc_match`, `knockout`, `stage`, `scheduled_days`, `match_id`), because DRS, tie-breaks and Test length depend on the event, the teams and the stage. It returns the extra fields listed under "Using it", and the earlier field names as deprecated aliases (`LEGACY_ALIASES`).
- `bowler_quota` is overs at full length; the reduced-match rule is the separate table `bowler_quota_reduced` (and `bowler_quota_balls` gives balls). Likewise `pp_windows` is the full-length window set and `pp_windows_reduced` the per-N table, in legal balls.
- `impact_player` is a boolean. The tactical-replacement kind is `tactical_sub` (`none`, `supersub`, `impact_player`); the BBL X-factor (2020/21-2021/22) and the ODI supersub (2005-06) are `tactical_sub = supersub` with `impact_player` false.
- `tie_break` has the plan's four values per match. The configured value may also be `knockout_only` (kept in `tie_break_rule`), which `rules_for` resolves by stage and date. Per deviation D5 the engines play `so_boundary_count` as a repeated super over; the 2015 Blast "lost fewer wickets" result has no value.
- `follow_on_lead` is one number (the scheduled length); the by-days rule is `follow_on_lead_by_days` plus `follow_on_threshold`.
- `pp_windows` uses 1-based over numbers in units of `balls_per_over`. For the Hundred that means 5-ball sets, so the 25-ball powerplay is sets 1-5.
- The plan's section 5 statement that T20 review limits went from 1 to 2 in 2019 is corrected by conflict 4 above. The plan's DRS fact list does not mention the host-board exceptions of conflict 21 or the women's Test top-up of conflict 22.
- `series_key` also splits the ICC Women's Championship and the NatWest Series by team pair, and applies the 60-day gap to tournament keys as well (a key gets a date suffix only when that splits it).
- Cricsheet match ids are not all integers: 25 files start `wi_`. Both modules key them as strings (`file_stem`), which matters for `matches.match_id` in plan 4.1.
- An extra derived file, `data/derived/cricket/competition-events.csv`, holds the full event list.
